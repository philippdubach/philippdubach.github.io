## Phase 1 -- SERP Analysis

### 1. Swiss sovereign AI

- **Format dominance:** Vendor landing pages, Swiss cloud/model services, infrastructure consulting, government or research documents, and a small number of general explainers.
- **Site types ranking:** Swiss providers, CSCS, consultancies, universities, government, and security/compliance platforms.
- **Content freshness:** High. Several Swiss services and documents were updated in 2026.
- **Common headings/sections:** Swiss hosting, jurisdiction, no training on customer data, audit trails, APIs, model choice, on-premise options, and regulated industries.
- **Gap:** Vendor pages describe features but rarely publish a transparent cluster-level revenue, cost, utilization, and payback scenario.
- **SERP features:** Product snippets, service FAQs, PDFs, and local-provider pages.

### 2. on-premise AI inference

- **Format dominance:** Cost/TCO guides, hardware-sizing guides, vendor white papers, deployment documentation, calculators, and practitioner discussions.
- **Site types ranking:** Hardware vendors, GPU clouds, infrastructure companies, academic repositories, and enterprise technology publishers.
- **Content freshness:** Very high because hardware, rental prices, and model efficiency change quickly.
- **Common headings/sections:** GPU selection, memory, interconnect, power, utilization, cloud comparison, total cost, support, and break-even.
- **Gap:** Most results focus on customer-owned equipment or much smaller models. The article examines a managed 64-GPU local service and the premium for control.
- **SERP features:** Calculators, pricing tables, PDF white papers, and direct cost answers.

### 3. private AI deployment

- **Format dominance:** Enterprise playbooks, deployment-option pages, private-cloud/on-prem comparisons, and implementation guides.
- **Site types ranking:** Model vendors, enterprise software companies, consultancies, GitHub resource lists, and publishers.
- **Content freshness:** High, with many 2026 guides.
- **Common headings/sections:** Definition, on-premise versus VPC, air gap, data governance, security, model control, hardware requirements, and operations.
- **Gap:** Many pages describe control benefits abstractly. The post asks what customers must pay for those benefits to support the infrastructure.
- **SERP features:** Definition snippets, comparison tables, and product documentation.

### 4. Swiss data residency

- **Format dominance:** Security/residency pages, legal reports, cloud services, regulated-industry product pages, and official guidance.
- **Site types ranking:** Swiss providers, CSCS, legal firms, government, global AI platforms, and compliance vendors.
- **Content freshness:** High because providers now distinguish storage residency from GPU inference residency.
- **Common headings/sections:** Storage location, inference location, logs, backups, support access, jurisdiction, subprocessors, and regulatory obligations.
- **Gap:** Marketing often treats location as full sovereignty. The post's product also includes audit access, continuity, exit, and a defined security boundary.
- **SERP features:** FAQ snippets, security checklists, and service pages.

### 5. local AI inference

- **Format dominance:** Technical explainers, inference APIs, hardware guides, self-hosting discussions, and provider landing pages.
- **Site types ranking:** IBM and other technology vendors, model-serving providers, Swiss consultancies, academic papers, and communities.
- **Content freshness:** High and technically volatile.
- **Common headings/sections:** What inference is, local versus cloud, privacy, GPU sizing, model serving, latency, throughput, and cost.
- **Gap:** Few pages distinguish local managed inference from a server on the customer's premises. “Put the model in the basement” makes that distinction memorable.
- **SERP features:** Definition snippets, code/API examples, and product results.

## Phase 5 -- Content Differentiation

The post provides a transparent hypothetical rather than a product pitch: $7 million initial cost, $370,000 monthly cost, 70% utilization, CHF 15,000-50,000 subscriptions, CHF 7.4 million revenue, CHF 3 million EBITDA, and three-year payback. Current Swiss provider pages explain residency and control, while generic infrastructure pages discuss TCO. Few combine both in a concise operator-level business case. The article also names the failure modes and refuses to equate local control with automatic security or compliance.

Do not add vendor comparisons or claim that the assumptions represent market quotes. The scenario's value is that readers can inspect the economics and sensitivities directly.

## Phase 8 -- Success Metrics

- **Target ranking:** Top ten is realistic for Swiss sovereign AI and 64-GPU/business-case long tails. Broad private-AI and on-premise-inference terms are vendor-heavy and competitive.
- **Competitive analysis:** Product pages lacking economic transparency are the first realistic pages to outrank for analytical queries. Providers remain stronger for transactional “buy” searches.
- **Content gaps to exploit:** Explicit operator economics, managed-local versus customer-owned distinction, control as the product, and clear failure conditions.
- **E-E-A-T assessment:** Strong analytical transparency and internal subject continuity. It does not claim operational experience with the hypothetical cluster, which is appropriate.
