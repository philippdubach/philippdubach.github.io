## Phase 1 -- SERP Analysis

Research combined four discovery searches with one focused search for each selected keyword. Results were reviewed qualitatively; no search-volume estimates were invented.

### 1. Bretton Woods III

- **Format dominance**: Concept explainers, investment commentary, archived research notes, podcast transcripts, and videos.
- **Site types ranking**: Research hosts, financial publishers, specialist commentary sites, and user-generated forums.
- **Content freshness**: The foundational material is from 2022. Newer explainers appeared in 2026, but many results still restate the original thesis without testing it.
- **Common headings/sections**: Inside versus outside money, commodity-backed currencies, sanctions and reserve freezes, gold, the renminbi, and dollar decline.
- **Gaps**: Few results provide a dated scorecard that separates durable mechanisms from failed or premature predictions.
- **SERP features**: Video results and definition-style explainers are visible; the query also surfaces the original paper and discussion transcripts.

### 2. de-dollarization

- **Format dominance**: Institutional explainers, market outlooks, news analysis, backgrounders, and videos.
- **Site types ranking**: Global banks, the Federal Reserve, the Council on Foreign Relations, publishers, and macro commentators.
- **Content freshness**: Strong. Several leading pages were updated in 2025 or 2026.
- **Common headings/sections**: Definition, causes, foreign-exchange reserves, trade invoicing, bond markets, commodities, sanctions, BRICS, and possible alternatives.
- **Gaps**: Many pages collapse a multidimensional process into one claim. The post distinguishes reserve holdings, transaction use, trade finance, and dollar-market depth.
- **SERP features**: Definition snippets, news modules, videos, and question-led subsections are common.

### 3. central bank gold buying

- **Format dominance**: Data dashboards, annual surveys, educational explainers, and market commentary.
- **Site types ranking**: The World Gold Council, central-banking publications, investment platforms, and market-data sites.
- **Content freshness**: Very high because reserve surveys and purchase data update quarterly or annually.
- **Common headings/sections**: Reasons for buying, largest buyers, reserve diversification, geopolitical risk, inflation hedging, custody, and measurement lags.
- **Gaps**: Commercial results often present gold buying as direct proof of de-dollarization. Federal Reserve research finds that relationship is not general across countries.
- **SERP features**: Data-rich result snippets, news coverage, and country tables.

### 4. dollar reserve currency

- **Format dominance**: Backgrounders, institutional research, policy commentary, and news analysis.
- **Site types ranking**: Federal Reserve, IMF-linked material, policy institutes, banks, government pages, and publishers.
- **Content freshness**: Mixed. Foundational backgrounders coexist with annual Federal Reserve updates and current news.
- **Common headings/sections**: Benefits of reserve status, reserve shares, Treasury holdings, trade invoicing, foreign-exchange use, alternatives, and de-dollarization.
- **Gaps**: Broad pages often discuss status in binary terms. The article's slow-diversification framing better matches the evidence across separate uses of the dollar.
- **SERP features**: Backgrounder snippets, news modules, and “is the dollar losing dominance?” question framing.

### 5. central bank gold reserves

- **Format dominance**: Official research, reserve datasets, annual surveys, and explanatory articles.
- **Site types ranking**: Federal Reserve, IMF, World Gold Council, Brookings, and financial publishers.
- **Content freshness**: High. Results include 2025 Federal Reserve research and 2026 World Gold Council and IMF material.
- **Common headings/sections**: Reserve-management objectives, country holdings, diversification, sanctions, storage, liquidity, and gold's limited transactional role.
- **Gaps**: The strongest opportunity is nuance: gold can diversify reserves without replacing the dollar as unit of account or medium of exchange.
- **SERP features**: Dataset links, PDF research, date-labelled surveys, and concise statistical snippets.

## Phase 5 -- Content Differentiation

The post's strongest differentiator is falsifiability. It uses a fixed October 26, 2025 evidence cutoff and separates later full-year figures. It also rejects the usual binary framing: reserve diversification can be real while the dollar remains dominant.

The article adds further depth through money-market plumbing, commodity collateral, cross-currency basis, and the boundary between nominal policy and real resources. Most ranking explainers cover the political narrative but not those mechanisms.

No Critical content gap requires a new section. Adding current 2026 data would change the article's dated evidence design and is outside this SEO pass.
