## Phase 1 -- SERP Analysis

Research combined four discovery searches with one focused search for each selected keyword. Findings are qualitative; no search-volume estimates were invented.

### 1. Wero payment system

- **Format dominance**: “What is Wero?” explainers, official FAQs, independent guides, bank help pages, and merchant-integration pages.
- **Site types ranking**: Wero/EPI, banks, payment processors, fintech publishers, documentation sites, and reference pages.
- **Content freshness**: Very high. Many results were published or refreshed in 2026 as e-commerce and bank integrations expanded.
- **Common headings/sections**: Definition, countries and banks, how transfers work, SEPA Instant, costs, security, merchant acceptance, rollout, and comparisons.
- **Gaps**: Most pages explain the product but not why EU fee regulation may have strengthened incumbent card networks or constrained entry economics.
- **SERP features**: Definition snippets, official support results, bank help pages, and current news.

### 2. European Payments Initiative

- **Format dominance**: Official pages, reference entries, bank announcements, policy documents, and history/rollout explainers.
- **Site types ranking**: EPI/Wero, banks, European policy organisations, publishers, and Wikipedia.
- **Content freshness**: Mixed. Foundational consortium pages rank beside 2025–2026 integration announcements.
- **Common headings/sections**: Shareholders, original card-scheme ambition, Wero pivot, national launches, iDEAL/Payconiq acquisitions, and EuroPA links.
- **Gaps**: Many results catalogue milestones without analysing capital requirements, governance failures, or the arbitrage created by existing public rails.
- **SERP features**: Knowledge-style summaries, press releases, PDFs, and institutional statements.

### 3. European payment sovereignty

- **Format dominance**: Policy briefs, central-bank speeches, news analysis, reports, and thought-leadership articles.
- **Site types ranking**: Central banks, European policy institutes, publishers, consultants, and financial institutions.
- **Content freshness**: High because geopolitical tensions and digital-euro legislation drove 2025–2026 coverage.
- **Common headings/sections**: Visa/Mastercard dependence, sanctions, strategic autonomy, digital euro, Wero, domestic schemes, resilience, and multi-rail models.
- **Gaps**: Policy debate can overstate a binary choice between foreign and sovereign rails. The article shows that Wero may still coexist with incumbent networks and even use their acquired A2A capabilities.
- **SERP features**: News modules, policy PDFs, video explainers, and quotation-rich snippets.

### 4. account-to-account payments Europe

- **Format dominance**: Product pages, regulator explainers, merchant guides, and white papers.
- **Site types ranking**: Mastercard, Visa, Worldline, the European Payments Council, regulators, and payment processors.
- **Content freshness**: High. Open-banking and instant-payment regulation keep the topic current.
- **Common headings/sections**: Direct bank transfer definition, real-time versus A2A, open banking, APIs, consent, settlement, merchant cost, fraud, and recurring payments.
- **Gaps**: Commercial pages rarely connect A2A economics with EU interchange regulation, scheme fees, and entry barriers for a pan-European network.
- **SERP features**: Definition snippets, product pages, PDFs, and implementation guides.

### 5. Wero vs Visa Mastercard

- **Format dominance**: Side-by-side merchant guides, long explainers, podcasts, videos, and current commentary.
- **Site types ranking**: Payment processors, fintech consultancies, niche publishers, podcasts, and the article's topic peers.
- **Content freshness**: Very high; several detailed comparison pages appeared in 2026.
- **Common headings/sections**: Rails, fees, settlement speed, chargebacks, consumer protection, acceptance, credit, rewards, geography, and roadmap.
- **Gaps**: Competitors often present Wero as simply cheaper. The article explains the regulatory paradox, network-scale problem, political subsidy case, and the risk that incumbent networks collect tolls on A2A rails.
- **SERP features**: Comparison tables, video/podcast results, merchant FAQs, and dated rollout sections.

## Phase 5 -- Content Differentiation

The article's differentiator is its infrastructure-arbitrage lens. It treats sovereignty as political support for a measurable merchant-cost gap rather than the sole economic rationale.

It also exposes a regulatory paradox: the interchange cap reduced one fee but encouraged scheme-fee substitution and left entrants with less revenue to fund network creation. The post combines this with governance history, bank capital, EuroPA's hub design, and the possibility that Visa or Mastercard process A2A payments themselves.

The fixed February 2026 evidence date is important. Newer SERPs report subsequent bank integrations and user totals, but adding them would change the article's publication-time scorecard and is outside this minimal SEO pass.
