## Phase 1 -- SERP Analysis

### 1. DORA critical ICT third-party providers

- **Format dominance:** Official designation pages, full-list explainers, oversight guides, and vendor-readiness articles.
- **Site types ranking:** EBA/EIOPA/ESMA, national supervisors, compliance publishers, consultancies, and vendors.
- **Content freshness:** High after the first official list on 18 November 2025; current pages focus on implementation and oversight.
- **Common headings/sections:** The 19 providers, designation method, Lead Overseer, inspection powers, fines, registers, and next steps.
- **Gaps:** Most list pages do not connect CTPP oversight with data sovereignty, US jurisdiction, and bank-level exit evidence.
- **SERP features:** Official PDFs, list snippets, FAQ blocks, and article tables.

### 2. DORA cloud exit strategy

- **Format dominance:** Guides, checklists, templates, vendor frameworks, and the ECB cloud-outsourcing guide.
- **Site types ranking:** ECB, cloud providers, consultancies, compliance vendors, and financial-services specialists.
- **Content freshness:** Mostly 2025-2026 because the ECB finalized its guide after DORA became applicable.
- **Common headings/sections:** Alternative providers, data portability, milestones, skills, cost, timing, testing, and contract termination.
- **Gaps:** Many pages give generic checklists. The post explains why geopolitical and jurisdictional risk make the exit plan substantive rather than procedural.
- **SERP features:** PDF extracts, how-to snippets, vendor guidance, and related compliance searches.

### 3. DORA third-party risk management

- **Format dominance:** Article-by-article guides, implementation checklists, registers, policy summaries, and software landing pages.
- **Site types ranking:** Supervisors, consultancies, compliance SaaS providers, banks, cloud providers, and specialist publishers.
- **Content freshness:** High, with post-application implementation guidance replacing pre-2025 readiness content.
- **Common headings/sections:** Due diligence, register of information, contracting, monitoring, incidents, subcontractors, concentration, and CTPP oversight.
- **Gaps:** Commercial results often treat compliance as a tooling exercise and understate the conflict-of-laws and supplier-market structure.
- **SERP features:** Guides, PDFs, video, FAQ sections, and templates.

### 4. EU data sovereignty CLOUD Act

- **Format dominance:** Legal explainers, cloud-provider assurances, policy analysis, and sovereign-cloud marketing.
- **Site types ranking:** Law firms, cloud vendors, policy organisations, publishers, and reference pages.
- **Content freshness:** Mixed. The 2018 CLOUD Act and 2020 Schrems II foundations coexist with 2025-2026 sovereign-cloud developments.
- **Common headings/sections:** Data location versus control, extraterritorial warrants, GDPR, Schrems II, encryption, corporate control, and EU-hosted subsidiaries.
- **Gaps:** Many results discuss personal data only. The post's use of EU Data Act Article 32 for non-personal bank data is more specific.
- **SERP features:** Video, explainers, discussion results, and sovereign-cloud product pages.

### 5. DORA concentration risk

- **Format dominance:** Official oversight pages, ECB guidance, bank-risk explainers, vendor frameworks, and academic analysis.
- **Site types ranking:** EBA, ECB, financial supervisors, consultancies, technology providers, and journals.
- **Content freshness:** High in 2025-2026 as supervisors move from adoption to effectiveness.
- **Common headings/sections:** Provider lock-in, substitutability, service and geographic concentration, subcontracting, systemic exposure, and exit testing.
- **Gaps:** Generic pages describe the risk but rarely give a decision framework that joins concentration metrics with jurisdiction and enforceable audit rights.
- **SERP features:** Official article links, PDF extracts, FAQ sections, and implementation commentary.
