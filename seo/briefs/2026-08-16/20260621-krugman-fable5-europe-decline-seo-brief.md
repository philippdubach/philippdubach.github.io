## Phase 1 -- SERP Analysis

### 1. European tech sovereignty

- **Format dominance:** Official policy pages, legislative documents, institutional reports, news analysis, and conference/video discussions.
- **Site types ranking:** European Commission, European Parliament, EUR-Lex, think tanks, academic publications, and major publishers.
- **Content freshness:** Very high after the June 2026 European Technology Sovereignty package.
- **Common headings/sections:** Definition, dependency on non-EU suppliers, chips, cloud, AI, open source, public procurement, investment, and strategic autonomy.
- **Gap:** Official pages explain programs but rarely quantify the entire dependency stack or test it against an actual model cutoff.
- **SERP features:** Institutional sitelinks, PDF/document results, news coverage, and videos.

### 2. European AI sovereignty

- **Format dominance:** Policy explainers, academic frameworks, infrastructure reports, industry commentary, and national-strategy discussions.
- **Site types ranking:** EU institutions, universities, consultancies, technology publishers, and research repositories.
- **Content freshness:** High; 2025-2026 documents dominate.
- **Common headings/sections:** Sovereign compute, frontier models, cloud jurisdiction, data, talent, regulation, open source, and competitiveness.
- **Gap:** Many pages treat sovereignty as residency or compliance. The essay treats it as control over every revocable layer of the stack.
- **SERP features:** Definition snippets, PDFs, news cards, and academic results.

### 3. AI kill switch Europe

- **Format dominance:** News, opinion, policy commentary, and community discussion.
- **Site types ranking:** Major newspapers, policy publications, EU sources, technology media, and forums.
- **Content freshness:** Extremely high around the June 2026 Fable 5 cutoff and the EU package.
- **Common headings/sections:** Foreign-provider dependency, service cutoff, US leverage, sanctions, sovereign cloud, and political reaction.
- **Gap:** Most coverage describes the incident. The essay ties the incident to the full chip-to-model stack and to a formal tail-risk framework.
- **SERP features:** News results and direct event summaries.

### 4. US AI export controls

- **Format dominance:** Official rules, policy analysis, legal commentary, news, and semiconductor explainers.
- **Site types ranking:** US government, legal/policy institutes, technology press, universities, and reference pages.
- **Content freshness:** High because controls and exemptions change frequently.
- **Common headings/sections:** AI chips, destination tiers, licensing, China, model weights, cloud access, and national-security authority.
- **Gap:** Chip restrictions dominate. The Fable case illustrates how export-control authority can affect a live API and allied users.
- **SERP features:** News, government documents, timelines, and definition results.

### 5. Europe US productivity gap

- **Format dominance:** Academic papers, IMF analysis, economist essays, and debate/rebuttal posts.
- **Site types ranking:** IMF, AEA, NBER, journals, Project Syndicate, and Paul Krugman's Substack.
- **Content freshness:** Mixed. Foundational work remains visible, but the 2026 measurement debate ranks prominently.
- **Common headings/sections:** Output per hour, constant versus current prices, PPP, industry mix, business dynamism, services, and living standards.
- **Gap:** Most results stop at economic measurement. The essay accepts much of Krugman's welfare case and separates it from strategic technology risk.
- **SERP features:** Academic cards, economist posts, PDFs, and news analysis.

## Phase 5 -- Content Differentiation

The essay accepts the strongest part of Krugman's living-standards argument and then changes the objective function from average welfare to tail loss. That is more precise than a generic “Europe is declining” piece. It also maps five layers—lithography, fabrication, accelerators, design software, and cloud/models—while acknowledging Europe's ASML leverage. The Fable event turns the abstraction into a dated operational example. Maintain the balanced concessions to open weights, ASML retaliation, and China's parallel stack.

Freshness note: the post explicitly reports conditions through June 21, 2026. Anthropic's [June 30 update](https://www.anthropic.com/news/redeploying-fable-5) says the controls were lifted and access was restored July 1. That later reversal does not erase the demonstrated revocability, but a future dated postscript could help readers arriving from current-event searches. It was not added during this SEO pass because the workflow prohibits changing substantive content or publication dates.
