## Phase 1 -- SERP Analysis

### 1. Kimi K3

- **Format dominance:** Official product/help pages, launch news, model cards, reviews, benchmark coverage, and unrelated product/entity results.
- **Site types ranking:** Moonshot/Kimi, news publishers, AI-tool sites, model-comparison publishers, arXiv, and reference pages.
- **Content freshness:** Extremely high because K3 launched in July 2026 and follow-on material is still appearing.
- **Common headings/sections:** Parameters, mixture of experts, context window, benchmarks, API price, open weights, coding, and comparisons.
- **Gap:** Most pages summarize vendor claims. The post supplies an actual 115-request session, detailed token mix, screenshots, and explicit limits.
- **SERP features:** News cards, academic results, comparison snippets, and entity ambiguity with Kia/K3 results.

### 2. Kimi K3 Claude Code

- **Format dominance:** Official integration docs, setup guides, community threads, and short implementation posts.
- **Site types ranking:** Kimi documentation, independent K3 sites, Reddit, and AI developer publishers.
- **Content freshness:** Very high; the official integration guide is current.
- **Common headings/sections:** API key, environment variables, base URL, model name, context options, routing, and troubleshooting.
- **Gap:** Setup pages explain how to connect the model but rarely report a sustained workflow, exported token data, subjective speed, or output evidence.
- **SERP features:** Documentation snippets, code/configuration blocks, and forum answers.

### 3. Kimi K3 review

- **Format dominance:** Review pages, benchmark roundups, news explainers, and videos.
- **Site types ranking:** Specialist AI publishers, technology news, tool directories, and individual reviewers.
- **Content freshness:** High and concentrated in July-August 2026.
- **Common headings/sections:** Specifications, benchmark table, pricing, strengths, limitations, Claude/GPT comparison, and verdict.
- **Gap:** Many reviews repeat the same launch table. This post distinguishes observed cost from fixed-token-mix estimates and makes its single-run limitation explicit.
- **SERP features:** Review snippets, comparison tables, news, and video results.

### 4. Kimi K3 vs Claude

- **Format dominance:** Head-to-head comparison pages, benchmark tables, practitioner tests, and news.
- **Site types ranking:** Comparison publishers, AI tools, technology media, and community discussions.
- **Content freshness:** Very high; competitors and prices change quickly.
- **Common headings/sections:** Coding scores, reasoning, speed, price per token, task cost, context, reliability, and best-use recommendations.
- **Gap:** Harness differences and token-volume differences often make nominal comparisons misleading. The post explicitly limits its calculation to one recorded traffic mix.
- **SERP features:** Tables, “which is better” snippets, and user discussions.

### 5. Kimi K3 open weights

- **Format dominance:** Official model cards, download/license guides, cloud deployment tutorials, news, and community hardware discussions.
- **Site types ranking:** Hugging Face, arXiv, AWS, technology media, independent guides, and forums.
- **Content freshness:** Extremely high after the July 27 release.
- **Common headings/sections:** Download, repository size, format, license, active parameters, quantisation, inference frameworks, and GPU requirements.
- **Gap:** Many pages treat availability as equivalent to practical self-hosting. The article emphasizes that open does not mean cheap and connects deployment to sovereignty and pricing power.
- **SERP features:** Model cards, academic cards, code blocks, and news results.

## Phase 3 -- Content Structure Analysis

### Recommended H1

I Tried Kimi K3 Inside Claude Code

Keep the H1. It is concise, first-hand, and perfectly aligned with the strongest long-tail query.

### Outline Comparison

Ranking reviews typically use specifications, benchmarks, pricing, pros/cons, and verdict. The post uses a stronger experiential sequence and then broadens into economics. Preserve it. Make only four headings more descriptive:

- “First impression in Claude Code: slow”
- “A quick Kimi K3 frontend test”
- “Kimi K3 cost in Claude Code”
- “Is Kimi K3 a Claude Opus replacement?”

Keep “So where do we go from here?”, “The threat,” “The opportunity,” and “So what?!” They preserve the essay's voice and distinguish it from template reviews.

### Introduction Assessment

- **Search intent:** The model, scale, architecture, availability, and test setup appear early.
- **Keyword placement:** Add a natural “Kimi K3 in Claude Code” sentence to the first paragraph.
- **Hook:** “I kept Claude Code and changed only the model behind it” is the core experimental design.

### Conclusion Assessment

- The final section gives a qualified verdict and restates cost, harness compatibility, and the threat to pricing power.
- The natural link to “Put the Model in the Basement” supplies the operational next step.
- A generic signup CTA in the body would be weaker than the existing article-level CTA and interlink.

## Phase 5 -- Content Differentiation

The post's strongest asset is evidence from one disclosed session: 115 requests, 13.64 million prompt tokens, 82,307 output tokens, 12.95 million cache hits, and a $7.18 bill. Screenshots show the frontend result. The author explicitly states that this is one run without a control or objective similarity score. That caveat is a credibility feature. The later analysis connects model substitutability to pricing power, capex, export controls, routing, and private deployment—far beyond repetitive benchmark-table reviews.

Freshness note: the article accurately reports the state on July 19, when Moonshot promised weights by July 27. The official [Hugging Face model card](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3/blob/main/README.md) and [technical paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24653) now confirm that the weights were released. A future dated postscript could clarify this for current searchers. It was not added because this SEO pass must not change substantive content or publication dates.

## Phase 7 -- Content Requirements

- **Target word count:** 2,000-3,500 words. The current long-form review and strategy essay fit this range.
- **Reading level:** Technical but carefully defined; preserve STE clarity and the short caveat sentences.
- **Tone:** Personal, skeptical, specific, and economically analytical.
- **Expertise signals present:** First-hand use, exported activity data, actual cost, external benchmark links, screenshots, explicit test limitations, list-price method, and dated availability claims.
- **Expertise signals missing:** Only a post-release weight-status note. Avoid adding new benchmark numbers unless they are evaluated under comparable harnesses.
- **Internal linking opportunities:** AI capex arms race; European sovereignty/export controls; Swiss local inference; enterprise AI economics; later model tests. Existing links cover the first three strongly.
- **External authority sources:** Moonshot launch and docs, official model card/technical paper, Arena, Artificial Analysis, Epoch AI, and the published price sources used in the calculation.

## Phase 8 -- Success Metrics

- **Target ranking:** Top ten is realistic for “Kimi K3 Claude Code” and first-hand cost/review long tails. Broad “Kimi K3” and “Kimi K3 vs Claude” terms are highly competitive and volatile.
- **Competitive analysis:** Template review sites and setup-only guides are the first realistic pages to outrank. Official docs will dominate configuration intent.
- **Content gaps to exploit:** Actual activity export, cache-aware cost, fixed-mix caveat, workflow/harness distinction, screenshots, and economic consequences.
- **E-E-A-T assessment:** Strong experience and transparency. The single-run limitation is disclosed rather than hidden.
